# Economic Standards & Commodity Price Index (ECONOMY.md)

## 1. Currency & Measurement Standards

### 1.1 Currencies (Eastern Han Dynasty)
- **Copper Coin (`Qian` / 文 / 钱)**: Primary transactional currency minted by the Han Court (Five-Zhu coin / Wuzhu Qian).
- **String of Cash (`Min` / `Guan` / 贯 / 缗)**: 1,000 copper coins strung together.
- **Gold (`Jin` / 斤金)**: 1 Han Jin (approx. 250 grams) of fine gold, nominally exchanged for **10,000 Qian** (fluctuates between 8,500 - 12,000 Qian depending on market liquidity).
- **Commodity Currency (Grain & Silk)**: Due to currency debasement and regional shortages, grain (Dan) and silk bolts (Pi) frequently function as hard money.

### 1.2 Weights & Measures
- **1 Dan (石 / Thạch)**: Dry volume measurement for grain, equivalent to 10 Dou (斗) ≈ 30 kg unhusked grain.
- **1 Pi (匹 / Thất)**: Standard bolt of plain silk, 40 Chi length by 2.2 Chi width (approx. 9.2m x 0.5m).
- **1 Jin (斤 / Cân)**: Eastern Han weight unit ≈ 250 grams (16 Liang / Lạng).
- **1 Li (里 / Dặm)**: Distance measure ≈ 415.8 meters.

## 4. Macroeconomic Modifiers by Region

- **Youzhou (Northern Frontier - Zhuo County, Ji)**: Abundant timber, horses, and leather. Grain costs slightly higher due to shorter growing season. Threat of nomadic raids keeps martial goods expensive.
- **Jizhou (Central Plains - Julu, Ye)**: Dense agricultural heartland. High grain production but currently disrupted by Taiping Dao mobilization. Banditry risk high.
- **Sili (Capital Region - Luoyang)**: Massive concentration of currency, high consumption of luxury goods, heavy imperial palace taxes.
